**Downstream assumptions that can change the scientific result.**

- [`Trajectory._interpolate()`](../reference/nvsl-analysis/src/analysis/trajectory.py#L126) defines missingness from x alone, saves the raw mask, linearly interpolates interior x/y/theta gaps, and holds the last position over terminal gaps. Leading gaps remain unresolved. Width/height are not interpolated. The executed loss-exclusion threshold is 10%, or 60% for `large2` (despite an outdated docstring), excluding startup loss; open-loop bypasses that check. Thus new coverage can also change which flies enter analyses.
- [`_calcDistances()`, `_calcSpeeds()`, `_setWalking()`](../reference/nvsl-analysis/src/analysis/trajectory.py#L220) use positional differences and nominal fps for speed; they do not generally substitute timestamp deltas. Interpolation changes distance, walking/rest decisions, and tortuosity; repeated frames and jitter matter. Floor pixel/mm calibration is not a 3D sidewall metric.
- [`_calcRewards()`](../reference/nvsl-analysis/src/analysis/trajectory.py#L2449) reconstructs circle entries but holds membership across original missing runs. Occupancy starts from a separate copy of the position-derived circle mask. Different metrics therefore treat gaps differently. [`_checkRewards()`](../reference/nvsl-analysis/src/analysis/trajectory.py#L431) compares reconstructed entries with recorded LED-on commands, tolerating some one-frame differences. Better agreement with recorded commands is not ground truth for better tracking: erroneous real-time tracking already influenced those commands.
- [`_calcAreas()` / `_setOnBottom()`](../reference/nvsl-analysis/src/analysis/trajectory.py#L250) use `πwh/4`, area/position heuristics for regular chambers, and essentially raw nonmissingness for large/HtL chambers. Recovering a large-chamber sidewall fly can therefore change presumed floor-valid coverage without solving floor/sidewall classification.
- [`EllipseToBoundaryDistCalculator`](../reference/nvsl-analysis/src/analysis/boundary_contact.pyx#L393) uses h/2 and w/2 and reoriented theta to calculate body-edge distances/contact events. A new ellipse fitter can move these events even when its center is unchanged. Head/tail resolution is motion-based inference, particularly uncertain for stationary or backward-walking flies.

Keep acquisition reward/stimulus history immutable. Label improved circle entries, crossings, and occupancy as reconstructed behavior. Validate these against manual behavior review rather than rewriting acquisition history.

**Plausible approaches, ordered by the evidence needed.**

These accuracy/runtime assessments are expectations, not measured performance. All require the same small manual benchmark.

| Approach | Expected benefit and remaining failures | Complexity/runtime | Annotation and validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole-recording robust background + normalized residuals + per-chamber components | Directly targets dark edges, startup contamination, moderate drift, stationary flies that move elsewhere sometime. Cannot reveal a fly occupying the same pixels throughout, absent contrast, or an occluded body. Lower thresholds can create well-edge/reflection detections. | Low; CPU-friendly, bounded background-sample memory; decode may dominate. | No training labels; easiest to inspect residuals and candidate acceptance. **First baseline.** |
| Same candidates + bidirectional association / per-chamber dynamic programming | Resolves competing objects and isolated false detections using both temporal directions; explicit missing state prevents forced paths. Does not repair absent image evidence. | Moderate; candidate storage and additional passes. For K candidates/frame, simple transitions cost O(NK²) per chamber. | Benchmark plus sequence labels; inspect disagreements and false continuous tracks. |
| Selective difficult-interval recovery | Retry alternative channels, lighting-state/local-window backgrounds, thresholds and robust shape fitting around gaps, jumps and ambiguity. Two-sided anchors can recover weak image-supported candidates. Risk of overfitting, body-outline drift and locking onto reflections. | Moderate; cheap if restricted to flagged intervals; maintain full-chamber reacquisition beyond a short local search. | Enrich hard cases and ordinary controls; evaluate at matched false-positive rates. |
| Learned chamber-crop segmentation + same association | May learn body appearance against sidewalls/reflections where intensity separation fails; supports outline/ellipse fitting. Domain shift, tiny objects and true invisibility remain. | Higher training/deployment cost; batch/GPU inference; measure end-to-end runtime. | Corrected masks, negatives, held-out recording/day splits, learning curves. Consider only if classical residual errors justify it. |

For background sampling, distribute frames across the entire recording, chamber, time and illumination states. Compare temporal median with a moderately high quantile for dark foreground; a max is overly sensitive to bright artifacts. Record sample counts and temporal variability. A global estimator assumes each pixel is visible without the fly sufficiently often and illumination is reasonably comparable. If violated, use blockwise/LED-conditioned estimates aligned to observed illumination, robust gain/offset correction outside candidate bodies, or masked second-pass estimation. Do not assume `.data` LED commands give exact optical transition frames.

Normalize the residual by local brightness/contrast and a robust noise estimate with a noise floor; use a low absolute signal floor to prevent amplification in near-black regions. Calibrate on development recordings. Use the complete chamber interior **and visible sidewalls**, with margins validated against imagery, rather than a floor-only mask. Preserve multiple plausible components until association; shape/size priors must tolerate foreshortening. Compare min-area-rectangle and robust body-ellipse fits against annotations before changing output shape semantics.

A second pass can mask confidently segmented bodies before refining backgrounds. Forward/backward disagreement should raise uncertainty; agreement alone is not independent evidence because both directions can track the same reflection. Motion models and optical flow can propose search regions, but neither validates an otherwise unsupported position. Start with per-chamber optimization; generic cross-fly assignment is unnecessary under the one-fly-per-compartment assumption.

**How Eggsactly and learned methods fit.** The [`splineDTorch README`](https://github.com/rcalfredson/splineDTorch/blob/main/README.md) describes single-category instance outlines, COCO segmentation annotations, augmentation and configurable U-Net backbones. Those are relevant capabilities, and your experience with data collection/quality control is reusable. [`Eggsactly`](https://github.com/rcalfredson/Eggsactly) separates chamber localization from egg detection; this project can similarly separate calibration from body detection. The existing egg model's reliability is your reported experience, not fly-domain validation; its weights should not be assumed transferable without evaluation.

With one fly per chamber, a compact binary segmenter such as a [U-Net](https://lmb.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/people/ronneber/u-net/) is a reasonable learned comparator to spline-based outlines; crowded-instance separation is not initially required. A pretrained prompted video segmenter such as [SAM 2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2) could assist annotation or be tested on representative crops. These are candidate tools, not evidence that either works on these small low-contrast flies, and SAM 2 is not unsupervised fly identification.

The practical way to reduce labels is to annotate diverse uncertain cases, use high-confidence classical masks only as reviewed proposals, and add augmentation/transfer learning. Pseudo-labels on easy frames alone can reproduce the classical detector's blind spots. Self-supervised pretraining might help with abundant unlabeled video, but is an additional experiment, not a reason to defer the small labeled benchmark. Never use neighboring frames from the same sequence as nominally independent training and test examples.

Introduce learning when manual review shows the fly remains visibly localizable but the tuned classical detector persistently confuses it with sidewalls/reflections or produces inaccurate outlines at the permitted false-detection rate. Use learning curves against annotation time to choose between compact segmentation and spline outlines. If humans cannot localize the fly reliably, preserve uncertainty rather than expect learning to manufacture evidence.

**Recommended first experiment and evaluation.**

Use the verified `c51` gap [4828,6968) as an initial development/review case, `c52` ordinary frames as controls, and the June recordings to diversify illumination and surface conditions. Do not designate an entire recording good or bad from NaN rate. Exclude the all-missing slot from recovery claims until occupancy is established. Add at least two more recording dates/cameras for a minimally credible held-out test; do not tune on all four inspected recordings.

First make a source manifest, audit decoded frame counts and event ranges, overlay chamber geometry, and review gap samples with temporal context. Then manually label approximately **600 fly-frame center targets**: half randomly sampled ordinary frames, half stratified difficult examples, plus **12 short contiguous sequences** covering loss onset/recovery, stationary bodies, dark edges/sidewalls, lighting changes and ambiguous reflections. Expand the sequence set if some categories are absent. Include finite-but-wrong candidate intervals; original NaNs cannot find these. Annotate approximately 100–150 diverse body masks/ellipses, marking legs/wings policy, body center, orientation modulo 180°, head only when visible, visibility/occlusion, sidewall status, and annotation uncertainty. Define the center from the visible body ellipse consistently, excluding detached reflections and extended legs/wings; adjudicate ambiguous postures. Double-label at least 20% to estimate human disagreement.

The existing [`gap_candidates()` / `add_observed_controls()`](../reference/nvsl-analysis/src/analysis/tracking_gap_review.py#L54) illustrate useful gap strata and controls, and [`tracking_gap_review_io.py`](../reference/nvsl-analysis/src/utils/tracking_gap_review_io.py) illustrates immutable review intervals. They are event-review infrastructure, not existing positional ground truth; reproduce only the necessary independent benchmark concepts here.

Split by recording/day before tuning; retain sampling probabilities to report representative ordinary-frame rates separately from enriched challenge results. Do not infer population rates directly from oversampled gaps. Repeated saved frames count for compatibility but must not inflate independent evidence; cluster uncertainty by recording/episode.

Compare: (A) original raw `.trx`; (B) independent candidate extraction with recording-wide background and original acquisition channel/threshold controls; (C) B plus normalized residual detection. Keep min-area-rectangle shape fitting fixed in this ablation. Evaluate temporal association and alternative ellipse fitting as separate later ablations. A legacy-like reimplementation on compressed saved video is not an exact reproduction of camera-time detection, so A remains the actual acquisition baseline.

Report the following at a declared localization tolerance, provisionally 2 saved-image pixels and also normalized by body length; revisit this tolerance based on human disagreement **before** freezing the held-out test:

- Center error: median, 95th percentile and full error distribution on labeled visible flies, separating originally finite frames and originally missing frames. Pair comparisons on common accepted frames with all-visible-frame correct-localization recall to prevent abstention from making error look artificially good.
- Missing rate over all slots/frames and occupied/visible opportunities separately; image-supported recovery recall among originally missing, manually localizable frames; false detections on empty/occluded opportunities and wrong-object/localization errors among emitted positions. Report the precision–coverage curve, not just its chosen threshold.
- Identity: wrong chamber/physical-slot assignments and transitions per reviewed sequence, distinguished from tracking the wrong object within the correct chamber. All-missing/absent slots are useful negatives once verified.
- Gaps: counts, median/95th/max lengths, remaining missing seconds and falsely bridged intervals; distinguish startup from active experiment phases. Use frame/fps for nominal video duration and original ts for source-time diagnostics, without resampling.
- Ellipse: center, major/minor full-diameter error, mask/ellipse overlap, and axis-angle error modulo 180°, stratified by sidewall/near-circular ambiguity. Score directed heading separately only when manually identifiable. Compare ordinary-frame shape quality as well as recovery.
- Runtime: wall-clock per video-hour, decode versus detection/background/association time, peak RAM, and GPU requirement on named hardware. No absolute runtime promise is supported yet.
- Downstream: compare distance, speed/walking, occupancy, reconstructed reward/control entries, rewards per distance, wall/agarose contact and turn events, gap exclusions and retained-fly counts under identical analysis settings. For annotated sequences measure event precision/recall and frame timing error. Keep recorded reward history fixed and report changed reconstructions without treating agreement with the real-time tracker as truth.
